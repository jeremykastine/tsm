Topic 28: Linear Equations with Fractions

Assigned to: TSM 098 and TSM 099

Overview

This topic solves linear equations with numerical fractions by multiplying every term by the least common denominator before continuing with ordinary linear-equation steps.

Key definitions

Least common denominator (LCD): the least common multiple of all numerical denominators. Clearing denominators: multiplying every term on both sides by the LCD. Multi-term numerator: a numerator containing a sum or difference.

Solve

\[\frac{5}{4}x + \frac{1}{8}x = \frac{3}{8} + x.\]

Fractions in a linear equation can be removed by multiplying every term on both sides by the least common multiple of the denominators. This is the multiplication property of equality, not cancellation across addition.

The LCM of \(4\) and \(8\) is \(8\). Multiply every term by \(8\).

\[8\left( \frac{5}{4}x \right) + 8\left( \frac{1}{8}x \right) = 8\left( \frac{3}{8} \right) + 8x\]

\[10x + x = 3 + 8x.\]

Now solve the resulting integer equation.

\[3x = 3\]

\[x = 1.\]

Solve

\[\frac{x + 7}{5} - \frac{19}{15} = \frac{x - 3}{6}.\]

The LCM is \(30\). Parentheses preserve each multi-term numerator when the equation is multiplied by \(30\).

\[6(x + 7) - 38 = 5(x - 3).\]

Distribute and solve.

\[6x + 4 = 5x - 15\]

\[x = - 19.\]

Check in the original fractional equation, not only in the cleared equation.

Sign care with negative denominators

If a denominator is negative, either keep the sign with the denominator during multiplication or rewrite the fraction with the negative sign in front. Do not lose the sign when the denominator cancels.

Solve and check: \(x/6 + 5/8 = 7/12\), \((x + 2)/8 = (x + 3)/( - 5)\), and \((2x - 1)/3 - (x + 4)/6 = 5/2\).
