Topic 1: Multiplication Tables and Divisibility Properties

Assigned to: TSM 098 and TSM 099

Overview

This topic builds multiplication-table fluency and uses divisibility properties to decide efficiently whether one whole number divides another without a remainder.

Key definitions

Divisible: a division has remainder 0. Factor: a whole number that divides another whole number evenly. Divisibility property: a shortcut for deciding divisibility from a number's digits.

\[24\]

Click to show solution

Twenty-four is divisible by two because it can be written as \(2\ \cdot \ 12\). That is, twenty-four contains exactly twelve groups of two, with nothing left over.

Now consider another number.

\[25\]

Click to show solution

Twenty-five is not divisible by two. Twelve groups of two account for twenty-four, leaving one extra. In other words, dividing twenty-five by two would produce a remainder of one.

Divisibility is a yes-or-no question about a whole number. When we ask whether a number is divisible by another number, we are asking whether it can be separated into equal groups with nothing left over. If nothing is left over, the answer is yes. If there is a remainder, the answer is no.

A divisibility property lets us answer that question without actually carrying out the division. Some properties depend only on the final digit. Others depend on the sum of the digits. These properties tell us whether division would come out evenly; they do not require us to find the quotient.

Determine whether the following number is divisible by two.

\[233,557\]

Click to show solution

A whole number is divisible by two when its final digit is even. The even digits are 0, 2, 4, 6, and 8. We do not need to inspect the other digits, and we do not need to perform the division. The final digit settles the question.

The final digit is seven. Seven is odd, so the number is not divisible by two.

Determine whether the following number is divisible by five.

\[418,980\]

Click to show solution

A whole number is divisible by five when its final digit is either zero or five. Again, the entire decision comes from the final digit.

The final digit is zero, so the number is divisible by five. The fact that the number also happens to be divisible by other numbers is not needed here; the question only asks about divisibility by five.

Determine whether the following number is divisible by ten.

\[665,926\]

Click to show solution

A whole number is divisible by ten only when its final digit is zero. Ending in five is enough for divisibility by five, but it is not enough for divisibility by ten.

The final digit is six rather than zero. The number is therefore not divisible by ten.

Determine whether the following number is divisible by three.

\[219,552\]

Click to show solution

Divisibility by three does not depend on the final digit. Add all of the digits of the number. If that digit sum is divisible by three, then the original number is divisible by three. The digit sum is only a test; it is not another expression with the same value as the original number.

The sum of the digits is \(2\ + \ 1\ + \ 9\ + \ 5\ + \ 5\ + \ 2\ = \ 24\). Twenty-four is divisible by three, so 219,552 is divisible by three.

Determine whether the following number is divisible by nine.

\[692,838\]

Click to show solution

The property for nine has the same form as the property for three. Add the digits. The original number is divisible by nine exactly when its digit sum is divisible by nine.

The sum of the digits is \(6\ + \ 9\ + \ 2\ + \ 8\ + \ 3\ + \ 8\ = \ 36\). Thirty-six is divisible by nine, so 692,838 is divisible by nine.

Now consider another number.

\[820,297\]

Click to show solution

The sum of the digits is \(8\ + \ 2\ + \ 0\ + \ 2\ + \ 9\ + \ 7\ = \ 28\). Twenty-eight is not divisible by nine, so 820,297 is not divisible by nine.

A number may be divisible by three without being divisible by nine. Passing the divisibility test for nine automatically means that the number also passes the test for three, but passing the test for three does not automatically mean that the number passes the test for nine.

Determine whether the following number is divisible by seven.

\[524,652\]

Click to show solution

The properties for 2, 3, 5, 9, and 10 let us settle the question without performing division. When no convenient property has been provided for the divisor, use long division and inspect the remainder.

Use long division to divide the number by seven. The quotient is 74,950 with a remainder of two. Because the remainder is not zero, 524,652 is not divisible by seven.

Use the smallest amount of work that settles the question. For 2, 5, and 10, inspect the final digit. For 3 and 9, inspect the digit sum. When no useful property is available, use long division and check the remainder. In every case, divisible means that nothing is left over.

Multiplication facts describe equal groups. For example, \(7 \cdot 8 = 56\) means seven groups of eight and also eight groups of seven. The commutative property lets one fact support the reversed fact.

Use known facts to build less familiar ones. Since \(5 \cdot 8 = 40\), one more group of eight gives \(6 \cdot 8 = 48\). Since \(10 \cdot 7 = 70\), subtract one group of seven to get \(9 \cdot 7 = 63\).

Division reverses multiplication. The fact \(6 \cdot 9 = 54\) also gives \(54 \div 6 = 9\) and \(54 \div 9 = 6\). This connection supports divisibility, fraction reduction, factoring, and equation solving.

Practice the facts through \(12 \cdot 12\), paying special attention to facts that are not yet automatic. For each multiplication fact, state both related division facts.
