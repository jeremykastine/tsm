Topic 32: Graphing in Slope-Intercept Form

Assigned to: TSM 099

Overview

This topic graphs a line directly from its slope and y-intercept and rewrites equations in slope-intercept form when necessary.

Key definitions

Slope-intercept form: y = mx + b. Slope m: vertical change divided by horizontal change. y-intercept b: the y-coordinate where the line crosses the y-axis.

Graph \(y = (7/4)x + 2\). Start at \((0,2)\). A slope of \(7/4\) means move right \(4\) and up \(7\), giving the point \((4,9)\). Moving left \(4\) and down \(7\) gives another point \(( - 4, - 5)\).

Read the slope and intercept directly

Slope-intercept form is

\[y = mx + b,\]

where \(m\) is the slope and \(b\) is the \(y\)-intercept. The intercept is the point \((0,b)\). The slope \(m\) is the ratio

\[m = \frac{\text{vertical change}}{\text{horizontal change}} = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}.\]

Graph \(y = - (1/2)x + 4\). Start at \((0,4)\). Interpret \(- 1/2\) as moving right \(2\) and down \(1\), producing \((2,3)\), \((4,2)\), and \((6,1)\). A line with negative slope falls from left to right.

Convert when necessary

To graph \(3x + 2y = 6\) by slope and intercept, isolate \(y\).

\[2y = - 3x + 6\]

\[y = - \frac{3}{2}x + 3.\]

The \(y\)-intercept is \((0,3)\), and the slope is \(- 3/2\).

Common errors

The intercept \(b\) is a point on the vertical axis, not a horizontal shift. Keep the numerator of the slope with the vertical change and the denominator with the horizontal change. A negative sign may be placed in the rise or the run, but not both.

Graph \(y = 2x - 3\), \(y = - (3/4)x + 5\), and \(x - 8y = - 8\). Choose points that remain inside a \(- 10\) to \(10\) window and check one point in each original equation.
